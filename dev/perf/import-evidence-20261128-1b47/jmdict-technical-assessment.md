# Why JMdict gains stayed small in this session

**The work that could be removed safely was often a small part of the import; several larger-looking opportunities moved work between phases or depended on an overlap window that the browser measurements did not quantify.** The remaining interventions needed to outperform their own admission/branching costs and the variation seen in identical-code controls. This explains these results; it is not evidence that JMdict has reached a performance limit. All findings below concern frozen baseline `9e10d3b3fcf66d344ab31bc42c9b522fa5215da2` and the separately identified candidates. No new source, test or measurement accompanies this assessment.

## Removed work: a real, small validator improvement

Restoring indexed score-key validation retained every check while removing iterator overhead. On the 12 captured JMdict buffers, the complete warm validator took median **2.559 ms in A, 0.985 ms in B and 2.587 ms in identical C**: approximately **1.57–1.60 ms saved across all 528,299 rows**. The remaining 0.99 ms includes header/view setup, so a more invasive validation bypass had little measured component headroom and would require new ownership guarantees across awaits. These are component measurements, not a promised import saving. [Immutable score evidence and reproduction](https://github.com/ManabiIO/manabitan/blob/3c3ba7600a8811df6b8631b72a0ac5379f8b60d1/dev/perf/import-evidence-20261128-1b47/score-restoration-README.md#frozen-identities-and-findings). The archive retains `evidence/score-validation/direct-validator-probe.json`; the [baseline record encoder and append boundaries](https://github.com/ManabiIO/manabitan/blob/9e10d3b3fcf66d344ab31bc42c9b522fa5215da2/ext/js/dictionary/term-record-opfs-store.js) show the ownership constraint.

## Moved work: the large close tail did not recur

The motivating **130.6 ms** record-close observation was one orientation run. In the repeated JMdict browser cohort, baseline close median was **8.65 ms**, and candidate close median was **4.30 ms**. Periodic checkpoints moved some flushing earlier; they did not eliminate the stored bytes or required final drain. Whole import was **−0.347% paired median, +0.175% equal-work**, with an interval spanning zero. A smaller inclusive tail therefore did not establish a smaller import. The separate memory-coverage failure is an additional qualification failure, not the reason the timing hypothesis failed. [Immutable browser addendum](https://github.com/ManabiIO/manabitan/blob/28332c2fc3a291f1728cd0441a878e3d5bd335ae/dev/perf/import-evidence-20261128-1b47/browser-results-addendum-README.md#local-checkpoints-hypothesis-not-supported). Its archived snapshots retain `evidence/checkpoint-final-disposition-1b47.md` and `.json`.

## Overlapped work: admission was real, useful overlap remains unmeasured

The pipeline changed ordering, not the amount of deduplication, compression or persistence. Every one of the 11 JMdict candidate browser reports entered the owned route for all 12 groups. Source review confirms that the early copy-completion promise survives every wrapper: there is no async custom-property loss. However, the candidate waits for record append and **all blocks'** source-consumed acknowledgements. Earlier jobs on each worker may already have compressed before its final acknowledgement, leaving a narrower potential window than the total compression duration. Message delivery and record work can narrow it further. The retained counters do not measure that window, and a deferral can be counted even when completion has already settled.

The complete result was **−0.226% paired median, +0.339% equal-work**, interval **[−0.989%, +2.274%]**. Thus the mechanism was admitted but did not demonstrate useful import acceleration. Neither aggregate phase sums nor the earlier Node timeline can supply a missing browser overlap duration. ## Removed lookups: the score-cache tradeoff failed its common-path gate

The repeat-only cache did remove substantial actual work: outlier `Map.get` calls fell **269,193 → 7,676**, and the complete warm JMdict encoder improved about **25–28%** against both baselines. Its median corpus times were A/B/C **12.018/8.337/11.248 ms**. That benefit is retained, even though the candidate was rejected.

On the fixed common-score controls, all-in-range wall time rose **5.24% versus A and 5.69% versus C**, with both intervals above zero. All four prospective common-path upper-bound checks failed. Moving every explicit new per-row operation into the outlier branch did not establish zero common-path cost; the data do not identify a specific JIT or code-layout cause. A few milliseconds saved on captured warm JMdict encoding did not justify this broader runtime change with unresolved first-use behavior and a demonstrated synthetic common-path penalty. The conditional cold study was never implemented or run. ## Native parsing: more reuse did not imply less elapsed time

The census found **11.31 million dispatched glossary string tokens**, averaging about nine bytes, but only about **108,000 rejected hash probes**. This made the scanner a more credible target than a table-tag redesign. Yet the reuse candidates had to pay for admission, exact equality and unsuccessful probes before avoiding syntax work. Raising JMdict glossary witnesses from **54,641** to **128,388**, **96,940** or **116,812** did not establish a gain against both identical baselines. In the delayed-hint study, the appealing **−4.39% B/A** inclusive median became **+2.78% B/C**, while identical C/A moved **−7.45%**. Witness counts establish activation, not net savings. [Immutable session evidence](https://github.com/ManabiIO/manabitan/blob/0df239a1be2cde2fc8586665413b7da7845f9fb6/dev/perf/import-evidence-20261128-1b47/session-held-README.md#findings-worth-retaining). The immediate-comma guard also removed identifiable compiled work but failed the separate 24-block confirmation: JMdict inclusive medians were **+1.14% B/A and −0.33% B/C**, with native and inclusive intervals crossing zero. Jitendex's apparent gain against C tracked unchanged inflation, weakening attribution to the guard. Small native changes therefore require both controls and phase attribution, even when their local instruction rationale is sound. ## Bounded implications for future review

Use the retained work counts, source hashes and complete observations to distinguish three questions before another proposal: **what work disappears, what earlier work replaces it, and which proven dependency keeps it on the import's critical path?** Treat orientation profiles as hypothesis generators; do not add overlapping phase durations, carry a one-run tail forward as a recurring budget, equate reuse/deferral counts with speed, or discard unfavorable controls. The approximately 1.6 ms validator result is an honest small gain; the VNDB CRC result is a separate corpus/mechanism, not evidence of a JMdict saving.

The next read-only step for overlap is to examine the existing [draft #977](https://github.com/ManabiIO/manabitan/pull/977), not duplicate it. Those are broader scheduling changes than this session's dedup-only overlap. Its body expressly limits the favorable codec evidence to a scheduling prototype, not the current PR runtime or browser import. Our flat result neither supplies its validation nor refutes it, and its numbers cannot be added to ours.
