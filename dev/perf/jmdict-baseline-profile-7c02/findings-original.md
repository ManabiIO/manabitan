# Why JMdict gains were difficult in this study

This explanation is limited to the frozen `9e10d3b3fcf66d344ab31bc42c9b522fa5215da2` source and the completed observations. It does not establish a general limit on future JMdict optimization.

1. **Much of the parser's work already overlaps other work.** The two ordinary hosted JMdict imports took **959.9 ms and 861.2 ms** for 528,299 rows. The exposed term-parse intervals were **196 ms and 210 ms**, whereas worker-reported native parser durations summed to **823 ms and 802 ms** across three concurrent workers. The latter are summed phase durations, not measured CPU time or elapsed import latency. Bulk-add terms took **561/452 ms**, including content-compression counters of **344.1/263.4 ms** and metadata publication of **152.2/116.9 ms**; finalization took **123/126 ms**, including record close of **108/112 ms**. These counters overlap and must not be added. **Inference:** a percentage reduction in summed parser work need not produce the same percentage reduction in import latency; storage/compression and exposed scheduling also matter. [Baseline acquisition](https://github.com/ManabiIO/manabitan/actions/runs/46102820124).

2. **An unchanged build produced changes larger than several apparent candidate gains.** The ordinary-before/ordinary-after JMdict comparison improved **10.28% with identical code**. That is observed host/order variation from two controls, not an estimated noise distribution or an optimization. The local reading/expression proof candidate showed **−4.924% wall** but only **4/6 faster pairs**, missing its prospectively required consistency. An earlier compression-context experiment also failed its identical control, which drifted **6.39%**. **Inference:** small, isolated positive comparisons were insufficient evidence for a PR; matched controls and fixed consistency gates materially affected the decision. [Immutable experiment ledger](https://github.com/ManabiIO/manabitan/blob/9ae4c4d09ec4b9ae1755f55032f9de6dd2abd207/dev/perf/zstd-sequence64-capacity-dispatch-7c02/investigation-ledger-snapshot.json).

3. **The trace identified several kinds of native work, and its weights were not removable milliseconds.** JMdict self-sample interval weights included **360.828 ms** in composite parsing, **223.066 ms** in native inflation and **159.718 ms** in CRC32. These weights sum overlapping profiles; signed negative deltas and long scheduling intervals remain in the original trace. They are neither process CPU measurements nor critical-path durations. The compact-score validator illustrates the limitation: its **26.265 ms** sampled weight contained **15.470 ms from only three intervals**. Its paired-validation experiment ultimately offered only about **0.4 ms additional CPU saving** over the existing indexed-loop comparator, while the enclosing constructor control failed. **Inference:** the trace justified inspecting that loop, but did not justify a 26 ms saving claim; the enclosing measurement and comparator governed the decision. [Baseline acquisition](https://github.com/ManabiIO/manabitan/actions/runs/46102820124); [experiment dispositions](https://github.com/ManabiIO/manabitan/blob/9ae4c4d09ec4b9ae1755f55032f9de6dd2abd207/dev/perf/zstd-sequence64-capacity-dispatch-7c02/investigation-ledger-snapshot.json).

4. **Several plausible shortcuts had little corpus opportunity or regressed after implementation.** The logical census found only **433 additional hits among 528,299 rows** for a four-distinct-definition cache, and **zero adjacent complete-tail matches** for whole-tail reuse. These are corpus opportunity counts, not measured performance. The strict-local parser prototype, even after restoring the baseline's lost helper inlining, regressed JMdict by **3.230% wall / 4.083% CPU**. The aligned-word checksum reader regressed its actual-JMdict checksum component by **105% CPU** versus A. **Inference:** fewer source-level operations, wider loads and new reuse proofs did not by themselves establish cheaper emitted/runtime work. [Immutable ledger](https://github.com/ManabiIO/manabitan/blob/9ae4c4d09ec4b9ae1755f55032f9de6dd2abd207/dev/perf/zstd-sequence64-capacity-dispatch-7c02/investigation-ledger-snapshot.json).

5. **The cleanest completed codec result was small even with stable controls and correct production geometry.** Before sequence64 timing, the corpus was corrected from the standalone 4 MiB target to the database's **1 MiB whole-entry packing: 103 blocks, 12 append groups**. The single completed 32-observation checked64 screen saved **2.65–4.85 ms of process CPU per canonical corpus**, or **1.5–2.8%**, across the three comparisons. Identical-control median CPU drift was **−0.204%**, and exact-rebuild/checked-in drift was **−0.520%**. Thus this particular rejection was not merely a noisy-host explanation: it missed the fixed **5%, 7/8-win and 10 ms** gain gates. The measurement was sequential codec work through four contexts, excluding workers, transport, allocation, storage and complete-import latency. The newer capacity-dispatch candidate has successful correctness/codegen evidence but **0/32 recorded timing observations**; its performance remains unknown. [Completed checked64 record](https://github.com/ManabiIO/manabitan/blob/aefc3ef11c00f4a87911b97757a7001fe75b2645/dev/perf/zstd-sequence64-rejected-7c02/RESULT.md); [incomplete capacity-dispatch record](https://github.com/ManabiIO/manabitan/blob/9ae4c4d09ec4b9ae1755f55032f9de6dd2abd207/dev/perf/zstd-sequence64-capacity-dispatch-7c02/RESULT.md).

The baseline's traced import took 1,349.3 ms and was explicitly non-authoritative for latency. Its two ordinary controls are the latency observations quoted above.
